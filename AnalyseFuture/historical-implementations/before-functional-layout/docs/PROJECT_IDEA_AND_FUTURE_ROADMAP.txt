DOMAIN RESELLER: A TRUSTED DIGITAL PRESENCE PLATFORM
Detailed project idea, delivery strategy and future research agenda
Planning date: 17 November 2026
Canonical project: https://github.com/renoukhan39-bit/domain-reseller

Purpose of this document
------------------------
Build on the substantial work already present. This is a product proposal and
planning document, not a claim that its proposed features are implemented,
commercially approved or ready for production. Existing acceptance criteria in
docs/TASKS.md remain release gates. Dates below are planning horizons measured
from a funded, staffed start, rather than delivery promises.

1. THE CENTRAL IDEA
-------------------
Create a trustworthy place where a person or small business can establish,
understand and maintain its online identity. Start with domain registration and
renewal through an accredited registrar's reseller API. Earn customer trust
through clear prices, accurate ownership records, reliable renewals, responsive
support and honest explanations when an external operation is uncertain.

The longer-term product brings domains, DNS, business email, website publishing
and selected hosting services into one customer account. Customers should see
what they own, what each service costs now and at renewal, who supplies it, and
what action is needed. Useful AI assistance can make that account easier to use.
Financial records and registry confirmations must remain authoritative.

The initial competitive position should be understandable pricing and dependable
service for a specific audience, not an immediate claim to match every feature
of GoDaddy, Namecheap or WHMCS. Choose an initial customer segment and language
through interviews. INR pricing exists in the current foundation; serving an
Indian audience is a plausible starting hypothesis, not completed market
research or a restriction against future regions.

2. WHAT HAS ALREADY BEEN BUILT
-----------------------------
Preserve the current Django/PostgreSQL application and its history. It already
contains accounts, email verification and resend, password reset, registrant
profiles, a TLD catalog, domain validation, an Openprovider adapter, Razorpay
Orders integration, captured-payment webhook validation, customer domain/order
pages, nameserver management, operator administration and audit records.

Additional existing work includes database-backed throttling, duplicate-event
protection, durable fulfillment claims, uncertain-operation escalation, payment
and refund reconciliation tooling, encrypted backup tooling, recovery drills,
database migrations, Docker Compose and a Python CI definition. These are
valuable foundations; rebuilding them merely to reorganize folders would lose
time and introduce risk.

Distinguish three evidence levels:

  IMPLEMENTED: Source, schema and configuration exist. This does not prove that
  a real provider accepts every request or that the deployed service is secure.

  LOCALLY VERIFIED: The earlier cloud onboarding in this conversation reported
  174 application tests on PostgreSQL, 17 backup-tool guard tests, an isolated
  synthetic-data encrypted restore drill, migrations, static asset generation,
  HTTP checks and service restart checks. These are prior local results, not a
  new test run performed by writing this document. They do not certify every
  future commit, production recovery or live financial correctness.

  EXTERNALLY UNVERIFIED: Real registrar sandbox contracts, actual Razorpay test
  webhook delivery, real SMTP delivery, staging deployment, jurisdictional
  compliance, operating procedures and live transactions still need evidence.

Sales and fulfillment remain disabled until their explicit approval gates pass.
Upstream PNLCS and WHMCS source preserved under AnalyseFuture/pnlcs-main and
AnalyseFuture/Openprovider-WHMCS-domains-master is reference material; its
presence does not add its features to this Django application. See
docs/REPOSITORY_STRUCTURE.md for the active and reference boundaries.

3. CUSTOMERS AND THEIR CORE PROBLEMS
-----------------------------------
First-time founders and independent professionals need help choosing a name,
understanding renewal costs, creating business email and avoiding accidental
service expiry. They benefit from a short, guided journey and plain language.

Small businesses need accurate ownership, invoices, reminders, continuity when
staff change and a clear support contact. Later organization roles should let
an employee manage DNS without allowing that employee to transfer ownership.

Agencies and freelancers need to manage several client portfolios while keeping
each client's ownership and billing separate. Delegated access and transparent
handover matter more than unrestricted access to all customer credentials.

Future resellers need scoped APIs, pricing tiers, account limits, idempotent
orders and reconciliation exports. This is a later business with contractual
and working-capital requirements, not a switch to enable during the first pilot.

Operators need a dependable view of captured payments, pending registrations,
uncertain provider results, refunds, approaching expiries and support incidents.
Operational clarity is part of the product, even when the customer never sees it.

4. THE FIRST COMPLETE CUSTOMER JOURNEY
-------------------------------------
A visitor searches an allowed domain. The platform validates the name and checks
the registrar. It displays the current registration price, renewal price, term,
tax treatment, relevant restrictions and the time of the availability result.
An availability result is a snapshot; another buyer may register the name first.

The customer creates an account, verifies email and supplies the registrant
details required for that TLD. The server creates an order from trusted pricing.
Checkout uses the payment provider's hosted flow. Browser success is shown as
payment processing until the server receives and validates authoritative payment
evidence. Never mark an order paid from a browser callback.

After a matching signed captured-payment event, a guarded worker submits the
registration once. It records provider identifiers and distinguishes pending,
active, failed and uncertain outcomes. The customer sees an honest status and
support route. An uncertain timeout triggers reconciliation before any retry.

The dashboard shows ownership details, expiry information obtained from the
provider, service status and permitted actions. Renewal and nameserver changes
require authenticated authorization. A customer can later export records or
request lawful correction/deletion, subject to registry and accounting duties.

5. PRODUCT MODULES AND DELIVERY PRIORITIES
-----------------------------------------
CORE COMMERCE: Harden the existing search, catalog, checkout, webhook and worker
path. Add real supplier-cost verification, quote expiration, transparent renewal
information and safe treatment of unsupported or premium domains. Unknown
supplier prices must block purchase rather than become invented fixed prices.

CUSTOMER ACCOUNT: Improve onboarding, accessibility, recovery, account-security
notifications and profile correction. Add organization roles and delegated
portfolio management only after tenant-isolation tests cover their boundaries.

DOMAIN LIFECYCLE: Add expiry synchronization, renewal reminders, eligibility
checks, transfers, DNS editing and DNSSEC in stages. Transfer locks, redemption
periods, registry restrictions and transfer ownership need dedicated workflows.

BILLING AND RECONCILIATION: Build invoices/tax handling with qualified review,
clear refund policies and an auditable transaction ledger. Reconcile gateway,
registrar and internal records. A receipt, captured payment, registrar debit,
settlement and completed refund are different events.

SUPPORT AND OPERATIONS: Start with a documented support process and incident
queue. Add customer tickets and knowledge articles when demand justifies them.
Reuse a maintained separate support product if it meets privacy and license
requirements, rather than automatically building another full support system.

EXPANSION: Evaluate email, hosting, website builder, certificates, reseller APIs
and multi-registrar routing after core reliability and margins are demonstrated.
Every expansion needs its own supplier, security, support and exit assessment.

6. ARCHITECTURE THAT RESPECTS EXISTING WORK
------------------------------------------
Keep the current Django modular application, PostgreSQL, server-rendered pages
and explicit worker boundary. Preserve working module paths during repository
cleanup. Group product concepts inside the application gradually when changes
are justified by tests and maintenance needs. Do not reorganize operational code
solely to make the folder tree look more fashionable.

PostgreSQL is the authority for customer entitlements, orders and durable work
claims. Provider APIs remain the authority for external facts such as domain
registration and expiry. If a queue or cache is added later, it must not become
the only record that money was captured or an irreversible request was attempted.

Separate application configuration, source, documentation, operations tools and
reference material. AnalyseFuture should preserve material whose relevance is
uncertain, with provenance and a reason for its placement. Preserved code is not
automatically trusted, installed or part of the deployed application.

Use one primary application foundation. PNLCS is a separate Laravel/PHP/MySQL
application, while this foundation is Django/Python/PostgreSQL. An adoption would
require an explicit architecture decision, migration plan and owner approval.
Extracting, studying or preserving PNLCS does not approve that migration.

7. REUSE, LICENSES AND THIRD-PARTY BOUNDARIES
-------------------------------------------
Classify each feature proposal before implementation:

  CONFIGURE an existing supported capability when sufficient.
  REUSE a maintained dependency or separate service with compatible terms.
  ADAPT existing project code after understanding its limits.
  BUILD a missing capability when the earlier choices do not meet the need.

Record the upstream source, version, license, notices, security concerns and
validation evidence. PNLCS is MIT-licensed and its notices must remain intact.
Do not assume the same license covers every other upstream project, bundled
dependency, trademark, asset or commercially supplied module.

Potential research sources already identified in this repository include
Panelica/PNLCS, FOSSBilling, Namingo and Upmind. These are comparisons, not an
endorsement of a specific version. The relevant links and license cautions are
in docs/REUSE_FIRST_STRATEGY.md and docs/OWNERSHIP_AND_LICENSES.md. The owner must
choose this project's own license and contribution policy with suitable advice.

Registrar API versions require contract testing. The existing adapter and current
compatibility document select Openprovider /v1; older references include /v1beta.
Real sandbox acceptance remains unverified. Do not replace URL strings without
checking schemas, sandbox support, authentication and responses.

8. FINANCIAL AND REGISTRAR SAFEGUARDS
-------------------------------------
The server calculates payable amounts. Payment events must match the intended
order, captured status, amount and currency, with signature verification and
duplicate handling. A changed price requires a new explicit customer decision.

External operations can succeed even when a local process times out. Design for
durable intent, idempotency where supported, operator review and reconciliation.
Do not promise mathematically universal exactly-once execution across external
systems. Test races, duplicates, crashes and partial failures explicitly.

Keep sandbox credentials isolated from live credentials. Never combine live
payments with sandbox registrations. Do not enable sales, fulfillment, live
keys, supplier funding or actual purchases merely because tests pass.

Registration ownership must use the customer's valid registrant data and policy
requirements, never a generic reseller owner handle. Nameserver edits, refunds,
transfers and ownership changes require defined authorization and audit trails.
High-impact administrative actions should have independent approval where the
operation and team size justify it.

Auto-renewal, if introduced, requires explicit opt-in, notice, a legally suitable
payment arrangement and clear cancellation rules. It must account for payment
failure, changed prices, currency movements, supplier downtime and registry cutoffs.

9. SECURITY, PRIVACY AND RECOVERY AS PRODUCT FEATURES
----------------------------------------------------
Use tenant isolation, CSRF protection, least-privilege roles, secure session
handling, dependency updates, rate limits and production edge protections. Add
strong operator authentication and review privileged access. Redact credentials,
registrant details and payment data from logs and model prompts.

Registry-required data collection is not permission to collect everything.
Document the purpose, lawful basis, retention, access and deletion exceptions for
each category. Customer deletion requests may require a controlled workflow
because accounting rules and registrar obligations can require retention.

Keep encrypted backups outside the primary failure domain. Define recovery-point
and recovery-time objectives from business impact, then prove them in a clean
staging recovery. A synthetic local restore drill is useful evidence, but it does
not prove the ability to restore the deployed service or recover after account
compromise.

A reported GitHub compromise also needs an owner-led account and organization
review: sessions, authentication factors, tokens, collaborators, deploy keys,
installed apps, Actions secrets/workflows and audit events. Repository cleanup
cannot revoke an attacker's access or certify the account is safe. Preserve
relevant evidence before destructive account or history changes. Rotate exposed
credentials through secure settings; never place them in this planning file.

10. OPERATIONS, OBSERVABILITY AND CUSTOMER TRUST
-----------------------------------------------
Monitor application health, database health, webhook lag, stuck orders, registrar
uncertainty, renewal risk, reconciliation differences and backup freshness. Alerts
need owners, escalation rules and a tested contact path. An unattended dashboard
is not an operating model.

Show understandable customer states: payment pending, processing, provider
pending, active, needs review or refunded. Do not display success because an
HTTP request was sent. Support staff should be able to explain what is known,
which evidence is missing and when the customer can expect an update.

Maintain incident procedures for provider outage, duplicate notifications,
unexpected charges, suspected account takeover, expiry risk and service rollback.
Preserve audit records during recovery. Define export and supplier-exit procedures
so customers are not trapped by a discontinued integration.

11. AI FEATURES THAT CAN HELP SOON
----------------------------------
DOMAIN DISCOVERY: Generate name candidates from a customer's business, language,
audience and style preferences. Check real availability separately. Explain why
a candidate fits, and warn that automated suggestions are not trademark advice.

GUIDED SETUP: Explain DNS records, email verification and domain lifecycle terms
using approved documentation and the customer's authorized service state. Give
the user a proposed plan and exact changes before a state-changing action.

SUPPORT DRAFTING: Retrieve approved knowledge articles, summarize an authorized
customer's issue and draft a support response for review. Escalate uncertainty,
account ownership disputes and financial decisions to people.

OPERATOR ASSISTANCE: Summarize already-recorded reconciliation evidence and help
prioritize an incident queue. AI suggestions must not alter the accounting
ledger, approve refunds, replay uncertain registrar writes or override access rules.

ENGINEERING ASSISTANCE: Help maintain tests, documentation and dependency reviews.
Treat model output, imported repositories, uploaded documents and tool responses
as untrusted input. Humans remain accountable for review and release decisions.

12. AI FEATURES TO EXPLORE AFTER ONE OR TWO YEARS
------------------------------------------------
At roughly 12-24 months, if core operations are reliable, evaluate portfolio
insights, multilingual support, assisted DNS diagnosis, explainable anomaly
detection and renewal-risk forecasting. Their usefulness depends on sufficient
lawfully collected data and measurable improvement over simple rules.

An AI website assistant could draft pages, accessibility improvements and
configuration plans from customer-approved content. Publishing should remain an
explicit customer action, with preview, version history, rollback and moderation.
It must not manufacture legal claims, reviews, business credentials or guarantees.

An agency assistant could prepare a plan for several client domains while
respecting organization boundaries. Delegation must use scoped authorization,
time limits, spend limits, audit records and revocation. Customer data belonging
to one tenant must never become another tenant's context.

For every AI experiment, set an owner, baseline, evaluation dataset, budget,
privacy assessment and stop condition. Measure incorrect advice and unsafe
action attempts as well as task completion. Version model/prompt configurations
and re-evaluate after changes. Better future models may improve performance,
but they cannot remove external service contracts or the need for consent.

13. WHAT CANNOT BE PROMISED
---------------------------
These are structural limits, not missing features to disguise:

  - AI cannot make an already-owned domain freely available, bypass registry
    rules, guarantee a trademark is safe or guarantee future availability.
  - No reseller can guarantee every domain registration, transfer or renewal
    succeeds instantly. Registries, eligibility rules and external outages apply.
  - No practical service can promise zero incidents, perfect fraud detection,
    perfect security, zero downtime or error-free AI output forever.
  - A payment and a registration cannot be made one atomic database transaction
    across independent external providers. Recovery and reconciliation are needed.
  - A repository reorganization cannot prove who accessed a GitHub account,
    recover every lost external record or eliminate an account compromise.
  - AI cannot grant registrar accreditation, business licenses, merchant approval,
    legal compliance or ownership of upstream intellectual property.
  - A model cannot lawfully or safely infer authority to spend customer money,
    transfer domains or disclose another customer's data from an ambiguous prompt.

Other ambitions are possible but require cost and effort: direct registrar
accreditation, operating authoritative DNS, global hosting, an email platform,
24-hour support and broad international compliance. They are not realistic
zero-cost additions to the first release. Reassess with specialist partners
rather than claiming that a better AI model will make them automatic.

14. DELIVERY HORIZONS AND EVIDENCE GATES
----------------------------------------
NOW TO THREE MONTHS: Establish a trusted repository baseline and inventory,
repeatable development environment and reviewed access controls. Finish the
core provider sandbox journey, real test webhooks, real email, billing policy,
staging recovery and required security checks. Deliver a controlled sandbox
demonstration. Exit only when acceptance evidence exists for each selected gate.

THREE TO TWELVE MONTHS: If supplier, merchant, legal and financial gates pass,
run a supervised small live pilot with explicit owner approval. Limit products
and volume. Build invoice handling, reminder workflows, reconciliation views,
support discipline and measured recovery procedures. Let customer feedback and
unit economics determine the next investment.

TWELVE TO TWENTY-FOUR MONTHS: Conditional on retained customers and healthy
operations, expand portfolio management, selected transfers/DNS features,
organization roles, scoped reseller APIs and a second registrar if justified.
Test useful AI assistance with evaluation and approval boundaries. Consider one
adjacent product, such as business email, instead of launching all adjacencies.

BEYOND TWENTY-FOUR MONTHS: Reassess a broader digital presence platform: website
publishing, more integrations, advanced multilingual assistance and wholesale
capabilities. Direct accreditation or owned infrastructure remains a separate
business case. Run small experiments and retire those without customer value.

The next one or two years are a chance to learn and attempt ambitious features,
not evidence that those features will become safe, profitable or technically
possible on a particular date. Staffing, contracts, capital and results govern
the schedule. Existing TASKS.md acceptance tests remain the concrete checklist.

15. WHO SHOULD ATTEMPT AND OWN THE WORK
---------------------------------------
PRODUCT OWNER/FOUNDER: Select the audience, business model, scope, budgets and
service promises; approve partners and live launch. The repository owner retains
control and must appoint accountable human operators. An AI assistant can help
execute work but cannot assume legal ownership or ongoing business responsibility.

TECHNICAL LEAD/BACKEND ENGINEERS: Preserve the working foundation, maintain
contracts and data models, test failure modes and deliver scoped capabilities.
Own architecture decisions and reject parallel rewrites without a business case.

QA/SECURITY REVIEWERS: Verify tenant isolation, payment tampering, replay,
concurrency, account recovery and provider uncertainty. Independently assess
critical changes before launch. External specialists can fill gaps in a small team.

OPERATIONS/ON-CALL OWNER: Run staging, monitoring, secrets, releases, backups,
recovery and incidents. This duty must have named coverage, not an assumption
that an AI agent will watch systems indefinitely.

FINANCE/LEGAL/COMPLIANCE ADVISERS: Validate taxes, invoices, refunds, contracts,
KYC/merchant requirements, privacy duties and the funding model. Support staff
handle customer disputes and escalate situations outside their authority.

DESIGN/ACCESSIBILITY SPECIALIST: Make prices, consent, status and renewal actions
understandable, including mobile and assistive-technology use.

AI/ML ENGINEER OR QUALIFIED PARTNER: Own evaluations, retrieval permissions,
model changes, prompt-injection defenses and AI cost controls when experiments
are funded. Every experiment also needs a product sponsor and security reviewer.

A small team may combine roles, but must still name who owns each decision and
who checks it. Recruit or contract expertise when the existing team cannot
independently validate a safety-critical area.

16. BUSINESS MODEL, COSTS AND MEASURES OF SUCCESS
------------------------------------------------
Start with sustainable margins on eligible domain registrations and renewals.
Later consider transparent managed-service plans, organization features or
reseller subscriptions if customers demonstrate willingness to pay. Do not rely
on hidden renewal increases, confusing bundles or artificial urgency.

Calculate contribution per order from actual retail revenue excluding collected
taxes, less supplier cost, payment fees, applicable currency conversion, expected
refund/chargeback exposure and variable support/infrastructure costs. Track
working capital separately: suppliers may require prefunding while gateway
settlement arrives later. A positive margin does not guarantee enough cash.

AI is a metered service cost, not free labor. Set per-account and total budgets,
cache safe public answers, choose models appropriate to the task and retain a
non-AI customer path. Cloud credits and software licenses do not pay registrar
inventory, taxes, merchant fees, specialist review or customer support forever.

Measure completed-and-reconciled orders, duplicate irreversible operations,
unresolved paid orders, renewal outcomes, restore drill results, support response,
customer retention and contribution margin. For AI, measure grounded answer
quality, permission failures, escalation usefulness and cost per successful task.
Set numerical targets after baselining real traffic and business requirements;
do not invent operating guarantees before measurement.

17. RELEASE ACCEPTANCE AND THE NEXT CONCRETE STEPS
-------------------------------------------------
Use the existing roadmap and 20 acceptance tests as the implementation backlog.
Before a public pilot, retain evidence for actual provider sandbox registration,
renewal and nameserver changes; captured-payment webhook delivery and replay;
tenant isolation; email delivery; financial reconciliation; staging restoration;
legal/customer policies; production security; support and rollback drills.

Keep required code changes, account-access remediation and commercial approval
as separate work items so a clean folder structure is not mistaken for resolving
them. Never label an unresolved release gate complete because documentation or
a local mock test exists.

The immediate sequence is: preserve and inventory the project; establish the
active Django boundary and AnalyseFuture reference area; record verification
evidence; close the next P0 integration gap; validate one complete customer
journey; review costs and compliance; and seek explicit approval for a limited
live pilot only when the evidence supports it.

The project's strongest future is a reliable service that customers understand
and trust. AI can make development, discovery and assistance more effective.
Accurate ownership, money handling, honest status and accountable operations
are what make that ambition a sustainable product.
